# Competitive pillar: what each prompt must produce (user brief, 2026-10-17)

The client is KSSL. Every judgement is made **from KSSL's point of view**. Language on every card is
**plain English**: short sentences, no jargon a non-specialist would trip on; a technical term only when it
is the actual name of the thing, explained in a few words the first time.

## 1. Overview: signals (Threat / Watch)

**Threat**: a move by a company that **directly rivals KSSL's portfolio**, in **KSSL's own markets (geo
presence)**, that is **likely to happen soon** and has **high impact**.

**Watch**: the same kind of move, but the impact is **indirect**, or will **take time**, or happens **outside
KSSL's markets**. A watch signal keeps a **timeline**. Later news on the same story is added to it, and a
watch can **escalate to a threat** when probability or impact rises.

**Risk matrix.** Every signal is scored on two axes:
- Y = **probability** that the move materialises (and how soon);
- X = **impact** on KSSL.

High on both means threat; low on both means watch. Show the position, not only the label.

**Impact is not only "competes with a product".** Think across:
- **revenue and orders**: lost tenders, price pressure;
- **market share / standing**: reputation, position with a customer, "preferred supplier" status;
- **supply chain**: a shared supplier, capacity booked, raw material, forgings, licences;
- **technology / capability gap**;
- **partnerships**: a partner of KSSL teaming with a rival;
- **policy / regulation / export approvals.**

**Signal drawer (when a signal is clicked):**
1. **Title.**
2. **Image**, if the source is a news article.
3. **At a glance.** Not crowded. One word or one short line each:
   - **Who** (the company that acts);
   - **What / How** (the move: contract, JV, test, delivery…);
   - **Impact on KSSL** (the dimension + level);
   - **Third angle** (the other party: customer / partner / country);
   - **Dates** (event date; expected next step).
4. **What the article says.** The event in plain English, the crux only.
5. **Why it matters to KSSL.** The KSSL lens: which product line, market, supplier or partner is touched, and how.
6. **Key points to remember.** Several (not one): the facts and numbers worth keeping, each one short.
7. **Source(s).**

Feedback on the previous outputs: language too difficult; "at a glance" read like a summary (crowded);
key points too few (often one).

## 2. Competitor Profile and Products
Company details and products are direct facts (from the client's Excel files); no prompt judgement.

## 3. Product Evaluation (matchups): like-for-like only
A pair is compared only when the two products are genuinely comparable:
1. **Same category**, then **same sub-type**. A handgun is not comparable with an assault rifle; a towed
   howitzer is not comparable with a tracked self-propelled gun.
2. **Same calibre** where calibre applies (guns, ammunition).
3. **Same size / weight class.** Two quad-rotor drones of very different size are not comparable, even if
   their spec labels match.

A pair that fails the gate is not shown.

## 4. Product news + product timeline
Product news types:
- programme;
- event / trial / test;
- ongoing research / development;
- sales / orders;
- deliveries / deployment;
- combat or front-line use;
- component / upgrade research.

A product keeps a **timeline** across news items. For example: sold to Poland → delivered to Poland →
used on the front line → new variant / component research. A news item goes to a product when that product
is involved in any way.

## 5. Partnerships
Types:
- JV;
- MoU;
- deal / contract;
- supplier / sub-supplier;
- licence / ToT;
- teaming for a bid.

For each partnership, analyse:
- what **value each company gets** (capability, market access, capacity, technology, financing);
- what it **means for KSSL**.

Look for **overlap**: the same supplier or partner also works with KSSL. Then check **what it supplies to
whom**. If it is the same component, say so: what it gives, how, and the risk or advantage for KSSL.

## 6. Geo footprint
**Layer 1, presence.** For each competitor and for KSSL:
- where it **sells** (and to whom);
- where it **manufactures**;
- where it **buys / sources** from.

**Layer 2, analysis.** Overlaps and anomalies. For example, KSSL and a competitor sell the same type of
product in the same country, KSSL's product has better specs or price, and yet the competitor still wins.
Explain the likely reasons (incumbency, offsets, financing, local partner, government-to-government deal,
delivery time) and what it means.

## 7. Projects and Competitor Moment
These are news told as a **timeline**: the sequence of steps of a programme or a competitor's moves, each
step dated and sourced, with what changed at each step.
